AGM vs GEL vs Lithium: Which Leisure Battery for a Campervan?

By OffroadWatt June 9, 2026 9 min read

The leisure battery is the single most expensive — and most misunderstood — part of a campervan electrical system. AGM, GEL and Lithium all sell in amp-hours, but a 100Ah label means three very different things in practice. This guide compares them on what actually matters off-grid: usable capacity, lifespan, weight and real cost per cycle.

The three chemistries at a glance

AGM and GEL are both sealed lead-acid batteries — cheap, proven, heavy. Lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) is a newer chemistry that costs more upfront but changes the maths completely. Here is the honest summary before we dig in:

AGM — the value workhorse

Absorbed Glass Mat batteries hold their electrolyte in a fibreglass separator. They are sealed, maintenance-free and handle high momentary current well — useful for starting an inverter or a water pump. For an occasional weekend setup on a tight budget, AGM is hard to beat on price per amp-hour.

  • Pros: cheapest entry, robust, tolerant of cold, good surge current.
  • Cons: only ~50% usable, ~500 cycles, heavy, slow to fully recharge the last 20%.
Tip: AGM hates being left partially charged. Get it back to 100% regularly or sulfation will quietly halve its life.

GEL — the heat-tolerant option

GEL batteries suspend the electrolyte in a silica gel. They tolerate heat and deep cycling slightly better than AGM and self-discharge slowly, which makes them popular in hot climates and for vehicles left parked for weeks. The trade-off is charging: GEL needs a lower, gentler charge voltage and is unforgiving of fast or high-current charging.

  • Pros: excellent heat tolerance, slightly more cycles than AGM, very low self-discharge.
  • Cons: slow charging, sensitive to the wrong charge profile, still only ~50% usable and heavy.

Lithium (LiFePO4) — the off-grid standard

LiFePO4 is now the default for full-time van life and serious off-grid setups. It delivers 80–90% of its rated capacity, charges several times faster, holds voltage almost flat until nearly empty, and survives 2000–4000+ cycles. At roughly a third of the weight of lead-acid, it also frees up payload — a real constraint on a 3.5-tonne motorhome.

  • Pros: highest usable capacity, longest life, lightest, fast charging, stable voltage.
  • Cons: highest upfront cost; needs low-temperature charge protection in winter; pairs best with a DC-DC charger and a lithium-aware solar controller.

Why 100Ah is never 100Ah

The number on the label is the nominal capacity. What you can safely use is the nominal capacity multiplied by the chemistry's depth of discharge (DoD). Drain below it and you damage the cells.

Usable Ah = Nominal Ah × Depth of Discharge
BatteryNominalDoDUsable Ah
AGM 100Ah10050%50
GEL 100Ah10050%50
LiFePO4 100Ah10080–90%80–90

This is why a single 100Ah lithium battery realistically replaces a 160–180Ah lead-acid bank. When you compare prices, compare cost per usable amp-hour, not per nameplate amp-hour.

The real cost: price per usable cycle

Lead-acid looks cheaper until you account for how little you can use and how often you replace it. Spread the purchase price over the usable energy each battery delivers across its whole life and the ranking flips.

Cost per usable cycle = Price ÷ (Cycles × Usable Ah)

A €180 AGM 100Ah (≈500 cycles × 50Ah usable) works out to roughly €0.0072 per usable Ah-cycle. An €800 LiFePO4 100Ah (≈3000 cycles × 85Ah usable) is about €0.0031 — well under half the cost over its life, before you even count the weight saved and the recharges avoided.

Rule of thumb: if your van is used more than a few weeks a year, lithium is usually cheaper per real kilowatt-hour over ten years.

Which one should you choose?

  • Occasional weekends, tight budget, mostly on hook-up: AGM. Cheap and good enough.
  • Hot climate, vehicle parked for long spells: GEL for its heat and standby tolerance.
  • Full-time travel, off-grid, a fridge running 24/7, an inverter: Lithium, every time. The usable capacity, lifespan and weight saving pay for themselves.

Whatever chemistry you lean toward, size it against your real daily consumption first — an oversized AGM bank is still only half usable, and an undersized lithium bank will still leave you flat.

Frequently asked questions

Is a lithium battery worth it for a campervan?

For full-time or frequent travel, yes. LiFePO4 gives roughly twice the usable capacity per amp-hour, lasts 4–6× more cycles and weighs a third of lead-acid. The higher purchase price is usually lower per usable cycle over ten years.

What is the difference between AGM and GEL batteries?

Both are sealed lead-acid. AGM holds electrolyte in a glass-mat separator and handles high current draws well; GEL uses a gelled electrolyte that tolerates heat and deep cycling slightly better but charges more slowly. Both are limited to about 50% usable depth of discharge.

How many amp-hours of lithium replace a 200Ah AGM battery?

A 200Ah AGM gives about 100Ah usable at 50% DoD. A 100Ah LiFePO4 battery delivers 80–90Ah usable, so roughly 120Ah of lithium matches the real usable capacity of a 200Ah AGM bank — at a third of the weight.
